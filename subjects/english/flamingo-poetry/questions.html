NCERT · Short
Q1. What will counting up to twelve and keeping still help us achieve?
Show answer

It will give us a pause from restless activity, so we can reflect and become aware of the harm our constant busyness causes to ourselves and to nature.

NCERT · Short
Q2. Do you think the poet advocates total inactivity and death?
Show answer

No. The poet says clearly that he has nothing to do with death. He wants a temporary, meaningful pause, not permanent inaction.

NCERT · Short
Q3. What symbol from Nature does the poet use to say that there can be life under apparent stillness?
Show answer

The Earth in winter. It seems dead but is actually alive underneath, which shows stillness does not mean the absence of life.

Extract-based · Practice
Q4. The poet mentions fishermen and whales, and a man gathering salt. What do these images suggest?
Show answer

In our daily rush we rarely notice the harm we do to nature or to ourselves. Stillness would let us become aware of it.

Long · 120-150 words
Q5. Keeping Quiet and Going Places show two contrasting ways individuals respond to inner conflict: silence and imagination. Discuss.
Show answer

In Keeping Quiet, Neruda advocates purposeful silence as self-reflection. Pausing restless activity helps people face inner conflict honestly, and leads to self-awareness and growth.

In contrast, Going Places shows imagination as an escape from reality. Sophie's fantasies reflect her dissatisfaction with her present life, but they lack practical grounding. Instead of resolving her inner conflict, imagination deepens her frustration and isolation.

Together, the texts show that silence used consciously promotes maturity, while unchecked imagination cut off from reality may hinder growth.

Answer structure: Para 1: Keeping Quiet, silence solves conflict. Para 2: Going Places, imagination deepens it. Para 3: Conclusion, contrast.
