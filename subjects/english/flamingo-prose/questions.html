The Last Lesson

NCERT · Short
Q1. What was Franz expected to be prepared with for school that day?
Show answer

Franz was expected to be ready on participles, because M. Hamel had said he would question the class on this grammar rule.

Hinglish: Participles padhke aana tha, kyunki teacher ne test lene ko bola tha.

NCERT · Short
Q2. What did Franz notice that was unusual about the school that day?
Show answer

The usual noise was missing and the school was completely still. Everyone was already seated, M. Hamel wore his formal green coat, and villagers sat quietly on the back benches.

NCERT · Short
Q3. What had been put up on the bulletin-board?
Show answer

The order from Berlin that only German would be taught in the schools of Alsace and Lorraine.

NCERT · Short
Q4. How did Franz's feelings about M. Hamel and school change?
Show answer

Franz used to fear M. Hamel and find books a burden. When he learnt it was the last lesson, he began to love his books and his teacher, and regretted wasting his time.

NCERT · Long
Q5. What message does M. Hamel give about the French language, and why?
Show answer

He calls French the most beautiful, clearest and most logical language. He says that when a people are enslaved, as long as they hold on to their language, it is as if they hold the key to their prison. Language is therefore a symbol of identity and freedom.

Extract-based · Practice
Q6. M. Hamel wrote "Vive La France!" on the board. (i) Why couldn't he speak? (ii) What did the act show?
Show answer

(i) He was overcome with emotion and could not get the words out. (ii) It was a silent, powerful expression of patriotism and protest. MCQ answer: silent patriotism.

Lost Spring

SQP · 40-50 words
Q1. Explain why the bangle makers of Firozabad are unable to imagine an alternative future despite their hardships.
Show answer

They are trapped by poverty, caste stigma and exploitative middlemen. Lack of education and fear of leaving the inherited trade make them accept suffering as destiny, so change feels impossible rather than something to work towards.

NCERT · Short
Q2. Is Saheb happy working at the tea-stall? Explain.
Show answer

No. He earns money and meals, but he has lost his carefree look because he is no longer his own master. The steel canister feels heavier than his plastic bag.

NCERT · Short
Q3. Mention the hazards of working in the glass bangles industry.
Show answer

Workers, including children, work near hot furnaces in dingy, airless cells. Many lose their eyesight early, and the work is illegal for children.

NCERT · Short
Q4. How is Mukesh's attitude different from his family's?
Show answer

His family accepts bangle-making as their fate. Mukesh dares to dream of becoming a motor mechanic and being his own master.

Irony · Practice
Q5. Saheb's full name means "lord of the universe". What is ironic here?
Show answer

He is a poor, barefoot ragpicker who does not even know what his name means. The grand name contrasts sharply with his reality.

Indigo

NCERT · Long
Q1. Why did Gandhi consider the Champaran episode a turning point in his life?
Show answer

It was his first successful satyagraha in India. It showed that ordinary Indians could resist injustice peacefully, freed the peasants from fear, and shaped his method for the rest of the freedom struggle.

NCERT · Short
Q2. How was Gandhi able to influence the lawyers?
Show answer

When Gandhi was ready to go to jail alone, the lawyers felt ashamed and chose to follow him instead of deserting the cause.

Extract-based · Practice
Q3. The amount of the refund mattered less than the landlords losing prestige. What was Gandhi's real aim?
Show answer

Not money, but breaking the hold of the planters' authority and proving that landlords could be challenged. That is why he accepted 25% instead of 50%.

Poets and Pancakes

NCERT · Short
Q1. Why was Kothamangalam Subbu considered No. 2 at Gemini Studios?
Show answer

He was multi-talented (poet, writer, actor) and quick with creative ideas, and he was very close and loyal to the Boss.

NCERT · Short
Q2. Why did the legal adviser lose his job?
Show answer

He belonged to the Story Department. When the Boss shut down the department and sent the poets home, he lost his job too, though he had nothing to do with poetry. The author calls this ironic.

Theme · Practice
Q3. What picture of Gemini Studios does the author give?
Show answer

A humorous one. Status and importance in the studio were often hollow, people worked in odd roles, and even visitors were welcomed without anyone knowing who they were.

Going Places

NCERT · Short
Q1. What were the differences between Sophie and Jansie?
Show answer

Sophie is a dreamer who imagines a glamorous future. Jansie is practical and accepts their limited prospects.

NCERT · Short
Q2. Why did Sophie like her brother Geoff more than anyone?
Show answer

Geoff stood for an exciting, unknown world beyond her reach. His silence and independence fascinated her.

NCERT · Long
Q3. Did Sophie really meet Danny Casey?
Show answer

No. The story strongly suggests it was imagination. She waits alone by the canal and plays the imagined meeting in her mind, and Danny never comes.

Cross-chapter Long Answer

SQP · 120-150 words
Indigo and Poets and Pancakes present power operating in very different contexts. Discuss.
Show answer

In Indigo, authority is exercised by British planters who force Champaran peasants into unfair indigo farming. Gandhi challenges it through moral courage, defying orders and accepting arrest, which exposes colonial injustice and brings real reform.

In Poets and Pancakes, authority at Gemini Studios works through hierarchy and status, not force. The author's gentle irony exposes how hollow it is, as when the legal adviser loses his job simply for sharing a department with poets.

Together, the texts show authority being challenged through ethical resistance (Indigo) or by exposing its emptiness through humour (Poets and Pancakes).
